  1. Buildings and infrastructure

    Data-driven concrete strength surrogates rank water–binder ratio as the dominant predictor

    Dataset-based ML surrogates (no new lab tests) rank w/b or w/c ratio first; test R² 0.88–0.96, and only the 3D-printed concrete model is externally checked.

    Editorial correction

    The recycled-aggregate study defines its leading predictor as water mass divided by cement mass. It did not have detailed binder composition for all 407 mixtures. The other two papers use total binder in the denominator. Two studies predict compressive strength, and the printed-concrete study predicts flexural strength. The ratios rank first within their respective models. UCI concrete model · Printed-concrete model · Recycled-aggregate model

    3 supporting papers

    All three studies train gradient-boosted surrogates on compiled mix-proportion datasets. None reports new laboratory casting. In each, SHAP-type attribution places water content relative to binder or cement first. On the UCI compressive-strength dataset, the eco-intelligent mix study's LightGBM reaches held-out R² 0.946 and MAE 2.639 MPa, with w/b dominant. Its Monte Carlo/Pareto screening finds 45 MPa candidates with a mean cement content of 164.4 kg/m³, 44.2% below empirical mixtures in the same strength band. The authors treat cement content only as a carbon surrogate, and the mixes are not validated. For flexural strength of 3D-printed concrete (209 records), CatBoost achieves 30-run R² 0.9302±0.0722 and seed-42 RMSE 1.761 MPa. W/B leads in SHAP, ALE and LIME. Validation on a source study withheld from training gives R² 0.769. For recycled aggregate concrete (407 mixes), LightGBM achieves R² 0.8822 and RMSE 4.0276 MPa. W/c ratio, sand ratio and superplasticizer dosage lead, and NSGA-II maps strength–cost–carbon Pareto fronts.

  2. Buildings and infrastructure

    Lab tests and reviews: moderate SCM or expansive-agent levels balance binder durability

    Bench study finds 6% expansive agent optimal in a 12% slag–fly ash–silica fume binder; reviews favour ~10% calcined sludge and flag sparse long-term data.

    Editorial correction

    The expansive-agent study measured strength, sulfate resistance and chloride transport in its own mixtures. The sludge review’s frequently reported replacement level pertains mainly to mechanical performance. The broader review identifies a lack of long-term durability evidence. Only the first paper directly compares durability across additive levels in one experiment. Binder experiment · Sludge review · Durability review

    3 supporting papers

    All three papers report that durability and strength are non-monotonic in replacement or additive level, and two relate this to pore structure. In a laboratory study (doi:10.3390/buildings16163169) of a binder with 12% slag–fly ash–silica fume replacement, a 6% expansive-agent dosage (A4) gave a 28-day compressive strength of 51.6 MPa, the highest sulfate resistance coefficient and the lowest chloride diffusion coefficient among the tested mixes. Excess expansive agent caused over-expansion and pore defects. A systematic review of water treatment sludge (doi:10.3390/buildings16163172) found that 10% calcined sludge, calcined at 600–800 °C, was most often reported as optimal, with dilution limiting C-S-H at higher levels. Durability data for sludge blends remain limited. A review of 48 low-carbon cement studies (doi:10.3390/buildings16163197) found no long-term or integrative durability studies. It links reactive fines to pore refinement and links extensive clinker replacement to lower alkaline reserve and greater carbonation susceptibility. No paper reports field or deployment data.

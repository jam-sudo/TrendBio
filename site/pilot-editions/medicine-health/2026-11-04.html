Research trends · 2026-11-04

  1. Health services and care delivery

    Readiness not linked to adoption; professionals report structural implementation barriers

    In 83 Swedish nurses, readiness showed no significant link to 19-week adoption; interview studies describe perceived structural barriers.

    3 supporting papers

    Across three health-services studies, organizational and structural conditions recur in descriptions of implementation, but the quantitative evidence is limited. The only quantitative test is an observational prospective study of Swedish child healthcare. Baseline E-Ready readiness was measured in 83 of 135 invited nurses from 37 centres and compared with registry-based MINISTOP adoption at 19 weeks. Negative binomial regression found no statistically significant associations.

    Readiness among colleagues (IRR 1.1, p=0.05) and workplace conditions (IRR 1.1, p=0.08) had positive but inconclusive estimates. The authors call for longitudinal studies with repeated readiness assessments. Two qualitative interview studies report perceptions rather than measured effects. Before implementation, 19 professionals in two Swedish ICUs, analysed with Normalisation Process Theory, described facilitators such as shared valuation, training and role clarity. They also described barriers including scarce resources, low patient volumes and documentation burden.

    Separately, 23 German heat-health professionals, analysed with COM-B, reported that capability and opportunity constraints, rather than motivation, limited heat-health action plan evaluation.

Research trends · 2026-11-04

  1. Galactic astronomy

    JWST spectra show suppressed star-formation phases are common and long-lived at z>4

    OASIS (JWST GO 5997) spectra characterize mini-quenched and lulling z>4 galaxies; a separate model forecasts spectroscopic tests of burstiness at z~14.

    4 supporting papers

    Three OASIS papers (JWST Cycle 3 GO 5997, deep NIRSpec/PRISM spectroscopy) probe the suppressed side of the burst cycle at z>4. Paper I identifies three mini-quenched galaxies (log M*=8.3–8.9, no detectable Balmer emission, at least 1.2–1.7 dex below the SFMS) and five lulling galaxies. In tiers not selected on star-formation state, suppressed galaxies make up 29% at z=4–5.5, or ~1.6×10^-3 Mpc^-3 dex^-1, which simulations predict to be an order of magnitude rarer. Paper II compares Balmer and 100-Myr SFRs for 54 galaxies and infers suppressed phases lasting ~100 Myr. It finds that [OIII]/[OII] tracks SFR_10/SFR_100 and interprets the declines as feedback-driven. Paper III (51 galaxies, ForcePho sizes, ppxf fits) reports that Hα/100-Myr burstiness anti-correlates with half-light radius, interpreted as an effect of central starbursts. Separately, a non-OASIS modeling study predicts that from z=10 to 14, increased burstiness roughly doubles the downturn fraction, whereas increased star formation efficiency keeps it constant.

    • OASIS I: Spectroscopy of Mini-Quenched and Lulling Galaxies Constrains the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4
    • OASIS II: The Ionized Gas Traces Feedback-Driven Declines into the Quiet Side of Bursty Star Formation at z > 4
    • OASIS III: Bursty star formation drives observed galaxy sizes at $z\geq4$
    • How bursty star formation shapes spectral diversity at high redshifts: breaking degeneracies in early galaxy formation

  2. Galactic astronomy

    Proposed ELT SHARP spectroscopy targets quiescent galaxies, transient hosts and UDG globulars

    Three white papers argue SHARP's AO-fed near-IR multiplexing (NEXUS, VESPER IFU) would enable observations current facilities cannot reach.

    3 supporting papers

    These three preprints are science-case papers for SHARP, a proposed near-infrared spectrograph for the ELT. None reports new detections; each argues feasibility or need. arXiv:2611.06331 states that elemental abundance patterns of massive quiescent galaxies at z~2-3 require S/N~20 per resolution element at ~0.1 arcsec resolution. It argues only SHARP-NEXUS with MORFEO-MCAO meets this, and proposes targeting overdensities and coordinating with star-forming surveys, citing 4MOST as a multiplex-sharing precedent. arXiv:2611.06966 claims NEXUS could characterize globular cluster populations in ultra-diffuse galaxies out to 60-70 Mpc in a few hours of ELT time. Its stated goal is radial velocities, stellar populations and dynamical masses to discriminate UDG formation scenarios. arXiv:2611.06352 is a review arguing that SHARP/VESPER IFU data could resolve metallicity, stellar age and kinematics at GRB, SN and kilonova explosion sites rather than relying on global host properties. All capabilities are forecasts.

    • Quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon: from individual element abundances to star formation histories
    • The Environment of High-Energy Transient Host Galaxies at High Redshifts as Observed by SHARP
    • SHARP(ening the) view of unresolved globular clusters in ultra diffuse galaxies

  3. Galactic astronomy

    AGN hosts trace links between black hole accretion, star formation and quenching

    Three preprints use JWST (SMILES/JADES, COSMOS-Web) and SDSS DR7 AGN host samples to test how AGN activity relates to host star formation, structure and quenching.

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints are observational studies of AGN host populations. arXiv:2610.41584 cross-matches 2,709 SMILES/JADES sources (0.05<z<6) and reports Pearson correlations of L_AGN with SFR and L_dust (~0.8) and M_star (~0.5). It attributes the L_AGN–age anti-correlation to cosmic time and interprets L_AGN as governed by cold-gas supply. arXiv:2611.06443 measures sizes of 3,955 AGN hosts (five diagnostics, 0<z<5) in COSMOS-Web imaging. It finds R_e∝(1+z)^-0.6, with some z>2.2 hosts suppressed yet extended, which it interprets as suppression preceding compaction. arXiv:2611.05353 applies MFICA decomposition to ~3000 SDSS DR7 Type-1/Type-2 hosts (0.16≤z≤0.4). It finds ~10% rapidly quenched post-starburst and slightly younger Type-2 populations. The authors offer covering-factor, host-obscuration or type-evolution explanations. These are interpretations, not established causes.

    • Co-evolution of AGN and Host Galaxies across Cosmic Time Using JWST data
    • Beyond just a torus? Re-contextualising the AGN type paradigm using data-driven host galaxy quenching histories
    • AGN Host Galaxies in COSMOS-Web: Linking Structural Evolution and Star Formation Quenching up to $z=5$

  1. Inorganic chemistry

    Structural control of emission in hybrid halide, zincophosphite and phosphonium solids

    Three new reports tune solid-state emission via halide substitution, S-donor linkers for Ln3+ uptake, and solvent-driven iodide relocation in crystals.

    Editorial correction

    The bismuth study compares bandgaps across three halides and reports broadband emission for the iodide film. The zincophosphite study changes the emission color by adjusting the ratio of adsorbed europium and terbium. The phosphonium study measures how the emission changes when solvent exchange moves iodide within the crystal. Bismuth halides · Zincophosphites · Phosphonium salt

    3 supporting papers

    The common measurement across these studies is photoluminescence in crystalline organic–inorganic solids, tuned by changing local structure. In PEA bismuth halides (inorgchem.6c03044), halide substitution alters connectivity: PEA4BiI7 and PEA4BiBr7 crystallize as isolated [BiX6] octahedra, whereas the chloride adopts PEA4Bi2Cl10 edge-sharing dimers. Optical measurements give the bandgap order Cl > Br > I, and DFT assigns the conduction-band edge to Bi–X states. Spin-coated PEA4BiI7 films are oriented and show broadband, excitation-dependent emission.

    In the NTOU-12 zincophosphites (inorgchem.6c04380), replacing O-donor dicarboxylates with thiophenedicarboxylate lowers Eu3+ uptake by 27–42% and Tb3+ uptake by 32–41%. The authors rationalize this with HSAB arguments. Adjusting the Eu/Tb ratio tunes the emission color, and NTOU-12a emits white light under 254 nm excitation. In a diphosphonium diiodide (jacs.6c12003), solvent exchange relocates iodide by 4.3 Å through anion−π+ contacts and triggers phase transitions. Emission switches among 1LE, 3LE and 3CT states, spanning 415–650 nm.

    The excited-state and noncovalent-interaction assignments rest on structural and theoretical analysis.

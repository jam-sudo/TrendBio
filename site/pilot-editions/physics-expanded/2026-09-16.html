  1. Particle physics

    Inelastic DM parameters inferred from LZ's single measured 248 keV recoil candidate

    Seven theory preprints report model-dependent benchmarks or parameter regions that reproduce LZ's single 248 keV nuclear-recoil candidate (2.6σ).

    7 supporting papers

    LZ reported a single nuclear-recoil candidate, quoted as 248±23±23 keV (2609.15714) with 2.6σ background-only significance (2609.10827). None of these preprints adds data. Each infers dark-matter parameters within its own model. Endothermic scenarios include a scalar in chiral U(1)B−L with Z′ collider prospects (2609.10827) and a hidden U(1)H dark-photon model with kinetic-mixing bounds (2609.15600). 2609.13038 uses the Scotogenic model and adds U(1)X isospin violation to address IceCube solar-capture tension. 2609.15742 finds inverse-seesaw NMSSM sneutrino points consistent with the constraints it considers. Freeze-in at low reheating temperature reproduces the event over a continuous region for a pseudo-Dirac fermion (2609.13130). It also underlies exothermic hadrophilic q1−q2 scattering chosen to evade indirect detection (2609.15714). In 2609.17412, the ALP-coupling Lorentz structure selects endothermic or exothermic scattering, and benchmark spectra peak near 250 keV. That study proposes annual modulation as a discriminator.

  2. Atomic and molecular physics

    GRASP2018 RCI plus second-order MBPT: claimed CSF reductions, unquantified in abstracts

    Companion preprints apply RCI+second-order MBPT in GRASP2018 to Cl III, Fe XV, Se III; each asserts CSF-space reduction without abstract-level figures.

    4 supporting papers

    These four preprints form one sequential methods series, not independent replications. Each extends the same relativistic configuration interaction (RCI) plus stationary second-order Rayleigh-Schrödinger MBPT scheme, in irreducible tensorial form, within GRASP2018, and later installments cite the earlier parts by Gaigalas, Rynkun and Kitovienė. The perturbative treatment covers core-valence correlation for Cl III (2609.15430), adds core correlation for Fe XV (2609.16761), and adds core-core correlation for Fe XV energy structure and E1 transition properties (2609.16835). It then adds valence-valence correlation for Se III (2609.16888). In each case, correlations not handled perturbatively remain in RCI, and the second-order estimates identify the most important CSFs. All four abstracts state that the approach significantly reduces the CSF space for complex atoms and ions. None reports reduction factors, accuracy against experiment, or computational timings at abstract level. The claimed efficiency gain is the authors' assertion, demonstrated through theoretical calculations rather than measurements.

  3. Quantum physics

    Theory tests higher-order effective Hamiltonians for periodically driven quantum systems

    Three theory studies (NMR, intense light-matter, Rydberg arrays) assess when higher-order effective Hamiltonians stay accurate; no new measurements reported.

    3 supporting papers

    All three works are theoretical. They ask how far beyond-leading-order effective Hamiltonians remain accurate for driven systems. The NMR preprint (arXiv:2609.17121) uses the Floquet–Magnus expansion to prove that Floquet theory and average Hamiltonian theory are equivalent, and gives the first four orders of each. It applies them to the Bloch–Siegert shift and to dipolar-coupled spins under magic-angle spinning. It recommends Floquet–Van Vleck with the kick operator, which it reports as about three times more accurate than average Hamiltonian theory. doi:10.1103/jkmy-8zwr reconciles quasidegenerate Rayleigh–Schrödinger perturbation theory with adiabatic elimination. Its benchmark is Floquet calculations, not experiment, which the method matches closely at high intensities. Its corrections make the effective-Hamiltonian eigenvectors nonorthogonal. doi:10.1103/pn45-bxlh derives higher-order high-frequency corrections for the WAHUHA protocol in Rydberg arrays. In that model, pulse timing enforces approximate magnetization conservation and trimers gain mobility. The authors estimate, rather than measure, that the trimer dynamics is within experimental reach.

  4. Quantum physics

    Theory: nonreciprocity, scars and bound states protect quantum battery energy

    Model-based preprints predict higher or persistent stored energy via nonreciprocal cascades, dissipatively selected scars, or photonic-crystal bound states.

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints are theoretical. They ask how quantum batteries can retain stored or extractable energy despite dissipation, and none reports an experiment. arXiv:2609.09690 argues that a prior Metelmann-Clerk master equation is inconsistent. Within a cascaded open-system formalism, it derives a regime-independent sixteenfold steady-state nonreciprocal energy advantage over reciprocal batteries, compared with the fourfold value reported in that earlier study. Battery-to-charger efficiency is fourfold under symmetric damping and depends on the dissipation asymmetry. arXiv:2609.11595 uses engineered local bond dissipation in an embedding-type scar chain and a generalized J1–J2 chain. This drives passive states into scar-supported manifolds, where single-copy ergotropy approaches the entropy-matched work bound. Weak dephasing gives stable charging, and a scar-breaking perturbation reduces work together with scar support. arXiv:2609.17367 derives exact non-Markovian dynamics for a charger-battery pair in a 3D anisotropic photonic crystal bath. With two bound states, the battery energy oscillates persistently; one bound state stabilizes extractable energy; with none, the energy decays completely.

  5. Gravitation and relativity

    Theory papers map how modification parameters shift photon spheres and shadow sizes

    Three analytic preprints compute photon-sphere and critical-orbit quantities for Dymnikova, black-bounce and Kalb-Ramond black holes against GR limits.

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints are theoretical. They derive how photon-sphere structure and critical impact parameters depend on parameters that deform Schwarzschild or Reissner-Nordström geometries. arXiv:2609.12194 derives EFT corrections, from non-minimal electromagnetic-curvature couplings, to the photon sphere and strong-deflection coefficients in the Dymnikova spacetime. It reports that these corrections grow as the Dymnikova parameter ℓ approaches its critical value, and it proposes, without testing, that strong lensing could separate Dymnikova from Schwarzschild. arXiv:2609.14728 finds that in a symmetric black bounce supported by an anisotropic fluid, the asymptotic shadow radius depends directly on the matter parameter. The bounce parameter enters only through the existence condition of the outer unstable photon orbit. Its Solar-System bounds are inferred from existing measurements through weak-field corrections that depend on the model. arXiv:2609.16471 gives analytic photon-sphere radius, critical impact parameter, capture cross section and Lyapunov exponent for charged Kalb-Ramond-(A)dS black holes. It reports that Λ changes the instability timescale while the Lorentz-violating parameter l deforms the photon-sphere instability non-trivially.

  6. Quantum physics

    Code performance under leakage, biased, crosstalk and GAD noise: three differing scaling results

    Three preprints test logical scaling under non-Pauli noise: leakage (hFC, simulated), circuit-level bias/crosstalk (surface codes), and GAD noise (PAQEC, post-selected).

    Editorial correction

    The three models use different noise channels. The ZX and ZY surface-code study varies the bias of Pauli-X errors and adds correlated XX crosstalk in a separate set of simulations. The honeycomb Floquet study models leakage. The five-qubit study evaluates generalized amplitude damping with post-selected recovery. Noise models in Table 1 · Leakage simulations · Damping model

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints ask how logical performance depends on physical noise strength when noise departs from independent Pauli errors, but they report different observables. 2609.10666 reports numerical simulations in which honeycomb Floquet code circuits under leakage show a waterfall regime, with logical error falling faster than distance-limited scaling; the comparison with same-distance walking surface codes is stated for that regime. 2609.10876 reports circuit-level threshold behavior for ZX and ZY surface codes under Pauli-X bias, with and without XX crosstalk: ZX X-memory thresholds rise with bias, ZX Z-memory thresholds fall and saturate, ZY Y-memory thresholds are nearly constant, and crosstalk lowers Z-memory thresholds. 2609.15924 introduces PAQEC and a five-qubit permutation-invariant code whose fidelity loss is quadratic in damping strength under probabilistic (post-selected) recovery. These results are not directly comparable.

  8. Cosmology

    Unmodelled lensing systematics bias S8/σ8, Ωm in simulated Stage-IV analyses; only one gives numbers

    Simulation studies find unmodelled systematics bias Stage-IV/LSST-like S8, σ8, Ωm; only the n(z) anisotropy paper quantifies it: σ8 −1.7σ, Ωm +1.4σ.

    Editorial correction

    All three studies quantify effects in simulations. The redshift-distribution study finds shifts of −1.7σ in σ8 and +1.4σ in Ωm for one LSST-like setup. The intrinsic-alignment paper reports biases up to about 12σ in some 15,000-square-degree simulations. The blending paper finds that changing the definition of overlap reduces the tension between two parameter contours from 1.6σ to 0.54σ. The studies use different survey assumptions and comparisons. Redshift distributions · Intrinsic-alignment discussion · Blending comparison

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints use simulated or forward-modelled data to ask how an unmodelled astrophysical or observational systematic biases cosmological parameters in Stage-IV/LSST-like analyses; none reports measurements on real survey data. The anisotropic n(z) paper gives the only quantified shifts in the supplied text: for an LSST-like setup with KiDS-like selection, σ8 is biased low by 1.7σ and Ωm high by 1.4σ, with high-redshift galaxy bias shifting up to ~4σ. KiDS-like and DES-like setups stay below 1σ. The MGLenS intrinsic-alignment study (nonlinear linear alignment model; f(R) and nDGP) reports major cosmology-sector biases when IA is ignored, with modified gravity relatively unaffected, and most can be flagged by poor goodness-of-fit. The abstract supplies no bias magnitudes. The DC2 blending paper, using a blending-entropy cut S_b<0.2 that removes about 25% of objects, reports reduced cluster-lensing biases and reduced σ8 tension, again without a numerical bias in the supplied text.

  9. Quantum physics

    Copy-count bounds for learning low-rank, stabilizer and sparse states, each in its own model

    Three preprints bound the copies needed to learn structured states: rank-r with t-sample joint measurements, stabilizer states, and k-sparse pure states.

    Editorial correction

    Theorem 1.2 gives an algorithm for sparse mixed states of rank above one with polynomial running time in the number of copies and qubits. The open question is whether a faster algorithm can approach the time needed to read those copies. The authors give nearly optimal copy complexity for mixed states and nearly optimal copy and time bounds for pure states. Theorem 1.2 and the open question

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints ask how many copies of a restricted quantum state are needed for learning or tomography, and each gives a different answer for its own state class and error criterion. arXiv:2609.10514 proves a Θ(dr/ε²·max{1,r/√t}) rate for rank-≤r states in trace norm, with joint measurements on at most t samples; the lower bound allows adaptive protocols and the matching upper bound is nonadaptive. arXiv:2609.10974 bounds the copies for identifying stabilizer states as n+⌈log₂(1/δ)⌉−3 ≤ L_δ(n) ≤ n+⌈log₂(1/δ)⌉+4, with a polynomial-time algorithm that saturates the bound, and derives a Clifford-unitary query count by corollary. arXiv:2609.12219 gives Õ(k/ε) copies and Õ(kn/ε) time for k-sparse pure states at fidelity 1−ε, and Õ(kr/ε) copies for rank-r mixed states. Its time complexity for r>1 remains open. The bounds are theoretical results, not experiments.

  10. Fluid dynamics

    Derived reduced 2D models for deep-water waves over background flows; checks are analytic or numerical

    Three preprints derive 2D reduced models for deep-water waves on background flows; Part I and the closure paper illustrate or validate them, Part II reports no test.

    Editorial correction

    Basile Gallet explicitly builds Part II on the equations he derived with Alexandre Tlili in Part I. The reduced surface-wave amplitude depends on two horizontal coordinates and couples to a three-dimensional background flow. Part II verifies conservation of wave action, energy and horizontal momentum for the inviscid model. The paper leaves numerical studies for future work. Part I · Coupled model · Conservation checks

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints derive spatially 2D reductions of the 3D deep-water wave problem; the results are theoretical and computational, not laboratory measurements. Part I (2609.11587) uses an equivalent solvability condition to obtain reduced equations for slow flows, including a Schrödinger equation driven by near-surface vorticity for narrow-band waves, illustrated by an analytical weak-scattering solution and numerical strong-scattering solutions. Part II (2609.11631), a companion to Part I (shared author name only), derives a two-way coupled nonlinear wave–Craik-Leibovich model with stated conservation properties; its abstract reports no numerical test. The free-surface closure paper (2609.12995) treats linear waves on steady 3D flows, validated against Euler eigenmodes in two test problems, and reports unstable steep 2D Gerstner waves.

  11. Optics and photonics

    Theory: vortex soliton stability in C_n photonic structures depends on geometry, charge and model

    Three theoretical studies, no experiments, find vortex-soliton stability set by lattice geometry, charge and nonlinearity, each in its own model.

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints are theoretical or numerical studies of vortex solitons in photonic structures with discrete rotational symmetry, and none reports an experiment. The answers differ by model. The disclination-fractal study (2609.09825) reports stable vortex-soliton arrays along domain walls, checked by linear stability analysis and perturbed-propagation simulations, while conventional disclination arrays were completely unstable. The star-network study (2609.13951) finds that stability depends nontrivially on topological charge and symmetry, with charges limited by the discrete rotational symmetry, unlike ring-like arrays. The quasi-phase-matched 3D crystal study (2609.14216) reports robust square C4 vortex droplets, mainly for S=1 and partially for S=2 and 4, obeying the anti-Vakhitov–Kolokolov criterion in the cubic-defocusing regime. The abstracts do not support a single general stability rule.

  12. Particle physics

    Model-dependent predictions for the 248 keV LZ xenon recoil from endothermic dark matter

    Three theory papers test endothermic scattering for LZ's 248 keV xenon event; each result depends on its model, halo and nuclear response, not on measurements.

    Editorial correction

    All three calculations use the energy of the reported LZ candidate. In the solar study, the predicted annihilation rate is compared with a model-specific IceCube neutrino constraint. NA64 provides a separate accelerator constraint on the benchmark’s mediator parameters. The authors predict 0.958 accepted LZ events using a reconstructed detector response. Solar analysis and accelerator constraint · LZ measurement

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints are theoretical studies of the single 248 keV LZ nuclear-recoil candidate; none reports a new measurement. 2609.09230 finds that elastic scattering with xenon nuclear cancellations (GCN and JJ55 shell models) gives features at 211 and 214 keV. It proposes Ar-to-Xe scale ratios and annual modulation as tests: 1.082 for endothermic scattering at 1 TeV versus 1.63–2.01 for the nuclear calculations. 2609.10636 builds a U(1)_{B-3L_τ} model with an 840 GeV scalar and 320 keV splitting, giving 0.958 accepted events in a public LZ-response reconstruction. Its solar-capture evolution lies below a model-specific IceCube proxy and the NA64 limit. 2609.15634 shows that isotope-resolved rates near the halo-speed cutoff depend on the high-speed halo and nuclear response, and it is not an LZ likelihood fit.

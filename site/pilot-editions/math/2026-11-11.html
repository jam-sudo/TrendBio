  1. Numerical analysis

    Biot-type finite elements: which hypotheses secure well-posedness and error estimates?

    Three November 2026 preprints prove well-posedness and a priori error bounds for Biot variants, each under coefficient, resonance or small-data hypotheses.

    Editorial correction

    The time-harmonic model requires a non-resonance condition for continuous well-posedness. Its discrete stability result also requires a sufficiently fine mesh. The multicontinuum study assumes suitable coefficients. The stationary coupled study assumes block stability and small data. Time-harmonic model · Multicontinuum model · Stationary coupled model

    3 supporting papers

    All three preprints answer yes, but each answer depends on formulation-specific hypotheses. 2611.15275 constructs a new macroscopic multicontinuum Biot model by Galerkin projection onto continuum-dependent spaces with first-order multiscale corrections. Under suitable coefficient assumptions, it proves well-posedness and energy stability, plus well-posedness, stability and a priori error estimates for a Galerkin FEM/backward Euler scheme; no rate is stated. Its high-contrast experiments compare against averaged fine-scale solutions.

    For the noncoercive time-harmonic three-field model, 2611.15495 proves continuous well-posedness under a non-resonance condition via the Fredholm alternative. Its stabilized equal-order FEM are stable only on sufficiently fine meshes, by a Schatz-type argument without discrete T-coercivity, with optimal-order energy-norm estimates confirmed numerically. For stationary Biot–Poisson–Nernst–Planck, 2611.15960 assumes block-stability and small data. It obtains Lamé-uniform poroelastic bounds, a priori rates for a Raviart–Thomas discretization, and a 2D a posteriori estimator.

    The estimator is reliable under extra smallness and locally efficient for polynomial boundary data.

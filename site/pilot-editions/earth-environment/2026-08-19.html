  1. Ecosystems and conservation

    Field tracking and mark–recapture quantify density, space use and habitat needs of threatened taxa

    Three new field studies from Peru, India and France supply population density, survival, habitat-selection and corridor estimates to guide site-level conservation.

    Editorial correction

    The Peruvian study describes the puma as Least Concern globally and Near Threatened in Peru. It estimated density from camera records. The Indian study tracked florican movements and the French study estimated toad survival and recruitment. The three studies inform local conservation using different outcomes. Peruvian puma study · Indian florican study · French toad study

    3 supporting papers

    The common thread is empirical, individual-based monitoring that produces quantitative population and habitat parameters for conservation planning. None of the three studies projects future change. In lowland southeastern Peru, camera trapping (263 stations, 25,730 trap nights) gave an SECR puma density of 1.012 individuals/100 km² (95% CI 0.448–2.286). Occupancy models favoured prey and jaguar indices over the measured anthropogenic covariates, and terrain ruggedness weakened the positive puma–jaguar association.

    In India, satellite telemetry of 12 lesser floricans tagged in 2020–2022 and tracked until 2025 showed that grassland was the most selected habitat in all seasons. Nighttime light reduced nocturnal use more strongly in residents than in migrants. The delineated flyway covered about 206,000 km², with a high-density corridor of about 60,000 km².

    In France, Pradel robust-design capture–mark–recapture of Pelobates cultripes at three sites estimated 200–350 active adults per season at Le Porge and Le Plan and 50–100 at Les Tours. Survival and recruitment were inversely ranked across sites, and the authors note that site and study period are confounded.

  2. Water resources and quality

    Groundwater–surface water hysteresis and decoupling shift under drought and warming

    Three HESS studies pair field hysteresis diagnostics with process models projecting stronger groundwater decoupling under snow-to-rain shifts and rising ET demand.

    3 supporting papers

    The common thread is nonequilibrium groundwater behaviour controlling surface-water response. One study is observational. Two years of high-resolution stream-stage and groundwater-level data, with chemical sampling, from a third-order Mosel tributary near Trier, Germany, show counterclockwise hysteresis during summer and drought. The h-Index correlates strongly with hydrological turnover, which the authors use to diagnose seasonal GW–SW connectivity and hyporheic expansion. The other two are model studies.

    DHSVM simulations of a 27 km² snowy volcanic watershed reproduce field-surveyed network elasticity. Their predicted network-length anomaly tracks measured stream ionic-concentration anomalies (r = −0.92). Under a partial snow-to-rain transition, the model projects significantly greater L–Q hysteresis at daily to monthly scales (p < 0.01). Controlled hillslope simulations with particle tracking project that warming and greening cause earlier collapse of intermediate-age flow paths and outflow dominated by very young water.

    These modeled shifts toward weaker buffering and shallower, faster cycling are projections, not observed changes.

  3. Water resources and quality

    LSTM runoff models tested for reconstruction, warmer-climate transfer and forecast bias

    New studies probe LSTM rainfall–runoff models as tools for European streamflow reconstruction, warmer-climate simulation and operational flood forecasting.

    Editorial correction

    EARLS used E-OBS weather observations to train its runoff model. BiasCast used E-OBS in its reanalysis baseline, alongside other weather products. This establishes shared inputs. An exact match of the training gauges or dates would require checking the individual records. The warmer-climate study used 918 catchments in central Europe and shares a coauthor, Corinna Frank, with EARLS. EARLS methods · BiasCast inputs · Warmer-climate study

    3 supporting papers

    All three studies assess LSTM-based rainfall–runoff modelling outside its training conditions. EARLS reconstructs daily streamflow, with uncertainty estimates, for more than 10 000 European basins over 1953–2023 using a single LSTM trained on more than 5000 basins. These are model reconstructions, not gauged observations, and were evaluated on two held-out basin sets.

    A differential split-sample test trained models on 1960–1990 and tested them on the warmer 2000–2023 period; the study region is not stated in the abstract. The LSTM was most accurate (median NSE 0.74–0.79) but overestimated simulated declines in mean and high flows relative to observations, whereas hybrid differentiable and HBV models matched the observed change signals more closely. All model types degraded more in snow-dominated catchments.

    BiasCast, using 451 Extended LamaH-CE basins, reported that median NSE fell from 0.58 with reanalysis forcing to 0.33 with forecast forcing. A Sequential Forecast LSTM recovered median NSE to 0.63, rising to 0.71 when recent discharge observations were added. No shared training data is identified across the studies, so dataset overlap is unknown.

  4. Atmosphere

    Multi-model studies probe how volcanic and engineered aerosols perturb stratospheric ozone

    Three August 2026 ACP multi-model studies on Pinatubo, Hunga and SAI scenarios find inter-model spread a key limit on attribution and projection.

    Editorial correction

    The Pinatubo comparison evaluated model estimates of stratospheric temperature against reanalysis. The scenario study and the Hunga eruption study evaluated ozone responses. All three compared model results, and several researchers contributed to more than one paper. Pinatubo temperature comparison · Ozone scenario comparison · Hunga ozone study

    3 supporting papers

    Three chemistry-climate multi-model assessments examine stratospheric responses to aerosol and water-vapour perturbations. The ISA-MIP/HErSEA Pinatubo-like experiments yield multi-model mean temperature anomalies that agree well with reanalyses, but inter-model differences generally exceed within-model differences across injection amounts and altitudes. The authors trace this spread to transport, radiative transfer, microphysics and aerosol size distributions.

    In CCMI-2022 SAI projections for 2025–2099 under a moderate emission scenario, five models simulate a global total column ozone decrease of at most ~10 DU in the first three decades relative to no-SAI. Tropical lower-stratospheric heating differs by up to 4 K between models, and dynamical effects dominate ozone anomalies late in the century, except in the lower polar stratosphere.

    For the 2022 Hunga eruption, simulations indicate the eruption contributed to observed low southern mid-latitude ozone in 2022. They show comparable chemical and dynamical contributions and model-dependent Antarctic vortex depletion. All three studies caution against attribution from individual or few models.
